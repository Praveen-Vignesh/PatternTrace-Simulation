Two questions, one instrument.

Can a machine tell a person from a script? Aim bots move in ways humans do not — no overshoot, no correction, no tremor. We are building a classifier that reads the difference.

And can it tell one person from another? That is the harder question, and the reason this is a research project rather than a product. Every session you play is a data point in it.

Nothing here is a demo. The trainer is real, the drills are real, and the telemetry it records is the study.

Precision Flick One target. Flick, click, repeat. static
Static Flicking Three to four targets held on screen. static
Dynamic Reflex One target at a time. Hit it before it vanishes. 400–1500 ms
Reactive Strafing Track a target that changes direction without warning. tracking
Target Switching Several moving targets. Destroy one, acquire the next. 2–5 targets

Play, and be part of the measurement.

Free, no download, runs in this browser. Sessions are 5, 10 or 15 minutes. What matters most to the study is coming back more than once — a single session tells us almost nothing.
